Which of the two jobs are you actually doing

This is not a tool that is better than Smartsheet at Smartsheet's job. It is a different job, and picking the wrong one is what makes people unhappy in month three.

Smartsheet is a general work-management platform built on the spreadsheet grid. If what your team needs is a flexible shared work surface — rows, columns, formulas, and the operational apparatus a business builds around them — that is what it is for, and Ingantt does not have it. There is no sheet, no cross-sheet formula, and no general-purpose work surface here. If you came looking for a cheaper Smartsheet, the honest answer is that this is not one.

Ingantt is a scheduling tool. It exists for the moment when the plan stops being a list and starts being a network: when the finish date has to be calculated from dependencies and durations rather than typed in, when a two-week supplier slip has to cascade through two hundred downstream tasks, when the same three people are booked at 120% and you need to see it before the deadline does, and when progress has to be measured against a baseline that was captured rather than remembered.

The practical test is what happens when a date changes. In a grid, a date is a value someone edits, and everything downstream of it stays where it is until a human notices. In a schedule, a date is an output of the network, and the critical path tells you which of the two hundred tasks actually mattered. If the second one is the question you are being asked, no amount of conditional formatting gets you there.
